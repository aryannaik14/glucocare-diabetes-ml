Understanding Diabetes

Learn how Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes differ, why they develop, and how they can be managed.

Type 1 Diabetes

An autoimmune condition in which the immune system destroys the insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas.

  • The body produces little or no insulin.
  • It is not caused by lifestyle choices.
  • Insulin treatment is essential.
  • Often diagnosed in children and young adults.
Main Focus

Type 2 Diabetes

A condition in which the body does not use insulin effectively and may eventually produce insufficient insulin.

  • Insulin resistance is a key feature.
  • Risk is influenced by genetics, age, and lifestyle.
  • Management involves nutrition and physical activity.
  • Early care reduces complications.
